# Working on cruciata

## Maintain this guide

After every major change, update this file with the resulting architecture, behavior, constraints, and relevant verification commands. Keep `TODO.md` current during multi-part work. Document the finished implementation rather than abandoned approaches.

## Project

`src/isometric-art.js` installs world art and the depth queue after `artwork.js`. `src/settlement-art.js` supplies lower, forward-facing architectural masters: Christian tiled gables, timber framing and Romanesque masonry contrast with Islamic plaster courtyard compounds, flat parapets, pointed arches, patterned friezes, domes and minarets. Buildings retain their physical footprints and no longer have circular shadows. Wall-mounted tower decks account for curtain height and participate in sprite keys. Trees are shorter. Curved organic models use dense, unoutlined surfaces; material seams remain intentional details.

`src/people-art.js` paints curved, shaded people with faces, chainmail, heraldry, equipment and faction clothing. Mounted knights use bent seated legs and fixed stirrups while the horse animates its own articulated gait; rider walk frames do not play the infantry leg cycle. Workers have wood, stone, gold, field, berry and construction tracks. Standards are 16 percent shorter and their physics constrains fabric above seven world units. `scripts/bake-art.js` generates trimmed, deduplicated local PNG pages and `assets/atlas/manifest.js`; run `npm start`, then `npm run art:bake` after changing source masters. `src/sprite-atlas.js` loads pages on demand with a 32-page target and 64-page hard limit, retaining recently used pages to prevent dense-view eviction cycles. Known poses keep their last ready image while another page decodes; only absent/failed assets and unusual legacy footprints use bounded local source caches. Curtains draw their own sprites directly so an outer cache cannot preserve an empty loading frame. Build verification checks every PNG and frame rectangle. No external requests or services are required. Captured architecture remains independent of ownership.

Sprite masters bake at three pixels per world unit. The baker streams completed 1024-pixel pages into a staged artifact directory and releases their canvas memory; publishing swaps the completed atlas directory and preserves the previous set under `artifacts/atlas-before-*`. Appending at a different resolution is rejected. Walking takes precedence over work poses and tools remain lowered during travel. Stationary harvesting, clearing, construction and repair read `workPhase`; movement reads `anim`, and attacks read `strike`. Rendering never advances these clocks. `tests/worker-art.spec.js` verifies visible stationary harvesting and the actual collecting-to-walking simulation transition. Attached guard towers use a square projected stone footing that meets curtain masonry in all eight headings; tower art inherits reinforced status from its attachment before forming sprite keys. Attached towers sort after their joining curtains so neighboring walls cannot cut through their roofs.

`beginFrame`/`flushFrame` collect upright assets with their canvas transforms and alpha, sort them by ground depth, then draw deferred health overlays. Rendering must not advance simulation, expose unseen units, or alter saved entities. Standalone portrait/asset renders execute immediately. Pass ground positions in entities even when drawing through a translated local context. Include full visual height in culling and fit portraits separately from physical footprints; caravans, standards and deployed machines need their own portrait scale. Static model sprites share a cache capped at 384 entries and 16 million pixels; poses and resolution participate in keys. Large capital sprite origins reserve space on both sides of the complete footprint.

Picking preserves ground hit areas and additionally tests raised visible silhouettes using a reusable one-pixel canvas and cached art. `hitStructure`, `hitUnit` and `hitResource` apply conservative bounds before pixel reads. Selection, gathering, escort commands and explicit attacks use these helpers; visibility is still checked against the entity's ground position. Siege range validation uses the selected enemy's ground position when clicking its raised artwork. Cosmetic height never changes damage, arrival slots, collision or exploration.

Artwork verification: `npx playwright test tests/frontal-art.spec.js tests/isometric-art.spec.js tests/render-work.spec.js tests/standards-and-joins.spec.js tests/horse-art.spec.js` covers faction/captured silhouettes, shadow removal, atlas animation reuse, launch/reload separation, source bounds, depth ordering, portraits, cloth, joins and off-camera suspension. `tests/horse-art.spec.js` checks that the mounted rider stays seated, the horse gait changes, and the baked cavalry atlas has distinct walking poses. `?bake` bypasses atlas lookup for source-master inspection and generation; it grants no simulation mutation hooks. `node scripts/preview-art.js` creates the asset board; `node scripts/audit-world-art.js` creates the in-game screenshot gallery, including all campaigns, harvesting phases, Christian and Islamic mounted close-ups, and all-direction guard-tower joins. `node scripts/profile-render.js` compares idle, animated and off-camera rendering. Latest atlas profile: 104 units and two engines stayed at a 16.7 ms median/P95 in all three modes; visible scenes made zero geometry renders, with 32 atlas pages resident (64-page/64M-pixel limits). `node scripts/profile-game.js` profiles crowded Jerusalem simulation. All source masters and generated pages ship locally.

This is a static browser canvas RTS. `index.html` supplies the interface; `src/game.js` owns simulation, input, selection, economy, combat, saves, and rendering. `src/navigation.js` provides budgeted navigation and obstacle connectivity. `src/menus.js` owns menu integration; `src/artwork.js`, `src/siege-art.js`, `src/fog.js`, and `src/audio.js` supply specialized systems. `scripts/build-static.js` produces `dist/`.

## Working rules

- Inspect existing changes before editing and preserve unrelated work.
- Keep navigation collision clearance and narrow passage routing consistent with physical movement. Share long-distance searches for army orders; avoid multiplying expensive searches by troop count.
- Derive visible commands from live owned selected entities and their capabilities. Validate the same eligibility when executing commands.
- Preserve save compatibility: initialize new optional fields for older saves and keep pre-existing generated gates playable.
- Buildings and troops use IDs in selections and saved control groups. Filter dead and unavailable entities when dispatching commands.
- Keep the game static and usable without backend services or external requests.

## Verification

Use `npm run check` for syntax and formatting, `npm run build` for static packaging, and focused Playwright tests for changed behavior. Browser mutation hooks are available only with `/?test`. Tests run serially with an eight-second local cooldown; prefer focused files or `--grep`. Add regression coverage for substantive simulation changes and avoid tests that only duplicate implementation.

## Army commands and contextual actions

Manual army orders split troops into nearby companies with clear ground between neighbors. Each formation stores one budgeted center-to-center route, including its origin so every member has a nearby route entry; troops consume waypoint copies and retain their individual arrival slots. `formationRoutes` is a transient lookup refreshed during formation updates. Terrain changes invalidate shared routes. Combat and worker jobs retain independent navigation.

Advance corner waypoints only when the next leg is visible. Cohesion must not pull troops across obstacles or away from final arrival slots. Troops use a stable local avoidance side to step around settled allies without additional long-distance searches. Transient formation lookups and building action turns reset when starting or loading a session.

The bottom commands contain capability-dependent stance, blessing, scouting, siege deployment, gate, wall upgrade, demolition, and cancellation actions. Stances use `stance-fight`, `stance-hold`, and `stance-stand-down` buttons. There is no stance dropdown or advance/hold command pair. Keyboard fighting advances remain available.

`selectedStructures` centralizes owned live building selection. `nextStructure` and `actionTurns` rotate training and building commands among eligible selected structures, one operation per click; full training queues are skipped. Multi-selection uses Shift-click, drag, Alt-drag, and saved control groups.

Player gates are timed building upgrades of two completed adjacent straight wall sections. `queueWallUpgrade` adds a `building-upgrade` queue entry; its `partnerId` and the second wall's `upgradeOwner` reserve both sections. The curtain remains intact until completion. Canceling from either section refunds the paid upgrade and releases the reservation; destruction or invalidation of the partner cancels the upgrade. Gate upgrades take 25 seconds; guard tower upgrades take 30 seconds. Costs use the resulting building's cost less the original wall investment (gate: 86 stone/49 wood; tower: 108 stone/137 wood).

`gateSegments` retains both original positions and connection arms for exact closed collision and neighboring wall attachment; `gateAngle` controls diagonal artwork. One completed gate replaces both wall IDs without changing unrelated selections. Guard towers retain their wall ID, health fraction, reinforced health, and `wallAttachment` for curtain joins; they use normal tower attacks. Queued progress and reservations are stored in ordinary saves. Generated and old saved gates keep their original rectangular behavior. Demolition uses ordinary damage/destruction, including rubble and capital defeat rules; protected city centers are excluded.

`tests/group-orders.spec.js` covers 80 troops in two route-sharing companies, movement through arrival, contextual commands, producer rotation including full queues, corner exclusions, timed wall upgrades, gate reservation cancellation, guard tower combat, and saved upgrade progress. Focused movement, stance, navigation, and building tests run in Chromium and Firefox.

## Prolonged sieges and expansion

New bastion walls remain `kind: "wall"` with `fortified: true`, preserving wall connectivity, navigation, upgrades, and older saves. They cost 48 stone/12 wood, have 11,000 health, and take 16 seconds per builder. `bastionwall` is the construction catalog key; worker construction uses that specification for fortified walls. Rams and ballistas train at siege workshops and carry siege damage through melee and projectile impacts respectively. Rams, ballistas, and permanent great engines have distinct canvas artwork. `greatengine` foundations cost 1,200 wood/650 stone/500 gold and take 180 villager-seconds; completed immobile engines launch heavy splash-damage boulders every 18 seconds between 320 and 2,200 units away. Selected engines display their range and can receive a specific enemy target by right-click. Heavy flight and impact have larger particles, sound and distance-scaled camera shake; friendly fire remains enabled.

`defensivePlots` supplies new generated settlements and AI per-base fortification plans with irregular layered bastion walls, segmented two-wall gates, attached guard towers, and corner towers. Campaign capitals have enlarged residential/military districts; their starting worker count remains six. Existing saved standalone gates retain their collision behavior, while segmented gates render the original gatehouse asset rotated to the wall direction. Enemy expansion spends ordinary resources on a town-center foundation near explored resource clusters, assigns existing villagers, and supports up to four completed bases. Each base receives its own fortification plan. Enemy recruitment is faster and invasion cooldown is 65 seconds; threats near any completed base trigger local defense.

Explicit unit attack orders set `manualTarget` and keep the target ID despite closer enemies or lost visibility. Tactical steering and archer kiting cannot distract locked attackers. AI trebuchets can pack and redeploy when their locked target leaves range; player trebuchets require manual deployment changes. Target destruction or a replacement order releases the lock. Selected attackers display a pulsing red reticle on their visible designated target; attack acknowledgment uses a locally synthesized three-note audio cue governed by effects settings. Ordinary single-unit movement has no rear-rank or cavalry offset; multi-unit orders retain individual formation slots.

Verification: `npm run check`, `npm run build`, `npx playwright test tests/siege-expansion.spec.js` and `npx playwright test tests/group-orders.spec.js`. The new suite covers target persistence, arrival for all mobile combat types, starting capital defenses/citizen count, permanent engine construction/damage, and paid enemy expansion.

AI perimeter towers retain a wall attachment and reinforced health. Fortification validation uses the actual segment footprint, so gates and towers can fill their intended contiguous openings. Builders survey unknown perimeter ground before constructing. Expansion garrisons are excluded from scout recruitment and invasion musters; losing their base releases that assignment. Invasion waves can overlap once a new healthy army has mustered, and both invasion and local defense formations use one shared budgeted route per army. Obstacle lookahead stops at a reachable destination, preventing steering from stopping builders short of construction contact points. Permanent engines require allied vision for autonomous bombardment; scouting and observation towers support their long-range fire.

Bastion wall upgrades deduct their own original masonry investment, including mixed curtain/bastion gate pairs, and retain reinforced health in the resulting gate. Wall placement previews display the selected wall type's actual resource cost. Generated gate segments store forward/backward connection arms, and collision falls back to the segment angle when loading segments with missing arms. Regression coverage also checks repeated invasion waves and closed/open generated gate passages.

Player formations containing cavalry or siege equipment use 56-unit slot spacing to accommodate their larger separation radius. Bastion segments retain the standard 32-unit construction footprint and show darker outer masonry. Construction and gate-health regression coverage exercises the actual drag placement and upgrade interface.

New bastion perimeters use distinct 18,000-health segmented bastion gates. Upgrading a pair containing bastion masonry uses the `bastiongate` specification: 260 stone/120 wood less the original walls, 40 seconds. Two bastion sections therefore cost 164 stone/96 wood to upgrade. The resulting entity remains `kind: "gate"` with `bastionGate: true`; it shares reservation, cancellation, gate controls, and two-section collision with ordinary gates. Existing curtain-wall upgrades and legacy gate saves retain their stored health and queued timing.

## Campaign scale and regional defenses

`campaignRules` version 2 stores difficulty-scaled objectives, assault intervals and forward posts. Old saves without this field retain their legacy objectives. `prepareCampaign` builds longer escort/supply routes, a winter castle with six surrounding camps, and huge late-campaign Jerusalem districts with three defensive rings and twelve deployed veteran trebuchets. `campaignPressure` dispatches real stationed troops using shared formation routes; it does not create free reinforcements. Escort orders remain active while their wagon survives.

`regionalAI` manages patrols and invasions for each completed enemy town center and uncaptured independent city. Enemy settlements use `enemyFog`; independent cities use saved `militiaFog`. Patrols explore, but invasion targets must be discovered. Independent city replacements train through ordinary queues, paid from a local resident-supported treasury. Main opponent gathering remains finite and unchanged. `stationCurtainArchers` distributes archers behind outer walls in Hold stance; fixed defenders are excluded from scout and invasion recruitment. Arrows can cross walls. Gate traffic opens a passage for friendly regional sorties.

City capitals are protected, large buildings. Any eligible player soldier reaching capital contact captures surviving residents, guards, structures and open gates immediately, with a one-time 1,800 food/2,400 wood/1,800 stone/2,200 gold treasury. Population ceiling is 520. New campaign citadel objectives do not trigger premature capital victory; true town-center destruction still does.

Curtain/bastion collision radii are 15/24. Surface distance, builder contact and segmented gate joins share those physical widths; construction anchors remain 32 units apart. Rendering uses flat midpoint seams and angular joins rather than rounded caps on every segment. Attached towers draw their connecting curtain beneath their footprint. Bastion stone and its distinct gatehouse are darker. Save fields remain optional.

Verification: `npx playwright test tests/campaign-scale.spec.js` checks both browsers for escort scale, difficulty, immediate rewarded capture, heavy impact, animated artwork, winter encirclement, Jerusalem defenses, explored invasion targets, thick gate connectivity, and archers firing over the curtain. ## Cloth, projected machinery, effects, and performance

Army standards use a transient WeakMap cloth state in artwork. A pinned three-dimensional mesh simulates inertia, drag, movement-driven turbulence, gravity, seven-unit ground clearance and sleeping after settling. Simulation advances cloth in bounded substeps (up to 90 Hz), not during rendering. Slim staffs are 44 world units high; shorter forked tails trail movement and settle clear of the ground. The complete Jerusalem cross maps through cloth triangles in source masters. Runtime atlas tracks are baked from this simulation, with moving flutter and an eight-second settling sequence; off-camera cloth remains suspended. Building flags and mill sails use shared pre-rendered frames.

`src/siege-art.js` owns projected timber, wheels, gears and machine masters. Great engines have a circular rotating chassis, lowered support hoop, elevated axle, counterweight and arm on a stationary round foundation. They are 30 percent smaller; restoring older engine saves updates their footprint. Rams and ballistas have lower frames; trebuchets share their materials, with setup tied to deployment state. `src/model-raster.js` provides per-pixel depth occlusion with WebGL acceleration and a software fallback for source baking. Machine poses use local atlas pages and a shared fallback LRU capped at 128 entries/eight million pixels. Launch and reload tracks have separate keys; mobile machinery has finite movement, attack and deployment poses. Sprite bounds include the complete arm sweep.

Completed owned great engines accept empty-ground `fireTarget` points or explicit `attackTarget` IDs by right-click, validate the same 320-2200 range, swivel before firing, and retain targets in saves. Selected targets have a ground crosshair. `cancelSiegeTargeting` clears selected live owned trebuchet/great-engine targets through the capability-dependent Cancel target button. Player trebuchets stay packed while moving and require Pack up before relocation; AI deployment remains automatic. Projectile render scale varies smoothly with arc height while damage radius remains fixed.

Six Kenney CC0 textures in `assets/particles/` provide tinted smoke, dirt, sparks, light and flames, with a cached 128-pixel tint atlas per color/texture. Construction, movement, destruction and siege impacts emit bounded particles (800 live maximum). Trails emit at previous flight positions and render before projectile heads. Missing optional sprite fields fall back to ordinary particles. Kenney-derived landing WAVs use low-pitched filtered recordings and smooth bass decays, with no generated noise layer. Siege impacts bypass quiet-voice saturation and can be heard within 3600 units; owned projectile impacts retain the firing team so fog checks do not silence them. Hidden hostile events remain hidden. All files and licenses ship locally.

Combat structure searches use faction-separated 256-unit buckets instead of scanning every building. Curtain connections use local 128-unit buckets; wall/gate collision caches connection arms and uses squared distances. Unit spatial buckets reuse arrays. AI fog preserves exact visibility/exploration without painting unused canvases, and known structures are refreshed when observable fields change. These caches remain transient and rebuild with terrain/session changes.

Navigation query options accept `priority` and `owner`. Clear player routes return immediately; player jobs precede background jobs, with reserved capacity in the 128-job queue. A preempted background generator restarts safely because A* scratch arrays are shared. Replacement owner requests and retired formations cancel obsolete searches. The shared three-millisecond frame budget remains. Formation arrival slots must have a clear local leg from the commanded ground, preventing slots across nearby walls while retaining one route per company.

## Responsiveness, curtain geometry and battle effects (polish pass)

**Order responsiveness.** `moveUnit` never leaves a commanded unit idle while a budgeted route computes: `provisionalPath` heads straight for the goal when the first 90 units are clear, and units waiting on a company route (`waitingForRoute`) do not queue private searches. `nav.urgent()` is true while any player-priority job is pending; `frame` then gives navigation 6 ms instead of 3 ms. A stall watchdog (`u.progress`) re-plans after 2.5 s without 14 units of headway, accepts arrival within 150 units of the slot, and `roomyGround` lifts wedged units to clear ground. `reserveSlot` uses a 24-unit hash (`slots.grid`) and skips blocked candidates, keeping 120-unit orders near 40 ms. Player plain move orders are committed: `combatAI` ignores `lastHit` and `moveUnit` skips kiting/tactical pulls (`committedMove`) until arrival; attack orders and stances behave as before. Fighters that stand still now repel overlapping neighbours (`crowding`). Never-wedged starts: the final pass of `startLevel` calls `roomyGround` for player units that fail the margin-8 check; `placeClear` resolves overlaps when the winter castle capital is enlarged.

**Curtain rendering.** `curtainLayout`/`wallModel` in `src/isometric-art.js` build each curtain from projected prisms in world units (arms are half-vectors to the neighbouring seam, so the old screen-space stroke that skewed diagonals is gone). Half-width follows collision (15 curtain / 24 bastion). Corner pieces reach back by that half-width, zero-length gate links run a 16-unit abutment from the link key's direction, merlons/lips/courses are suppressed where another piece covers them, and the sprite key is versioned `curtain2:`. Curtain sprites render at runtime under the versioned key and cost about 1.5 ms when cold (cached afterwards), so no rebake is required; a future `npm run art:bake` will also bake them. `node scripts/audit-world-art.js "^(08-|08a|09|10)"` captures the eight-heading joins; `node scripts/shot-walls.js <level>` captures real campaign walls.

**Battle effects (cosmetic, bounded).** `strikeEffect` (sparks/dust, ring on a cavalry charge), `launchEffect` (siege plumes), `spawnDebris` (arcing masonry with a bounce, `state.debris`, 220 max), scorch decals (`state.marks` with `kind: "scorch"`), arrow heads/shadows, impact puffs, and smoke/flames on structures under 45 percent health (particles stay under 600). A cavalry charge (170+ units of run-up within 0.5 s of striking, `u.runUp`) deals 1.6x once. Selected troops draw dashed order lines and goal ticks. Effects never alter gameplay state beyond the charge multiplier.

**Test and debugging hooks (`?test`).** `cruciata.liveFrame(n)` emulates production frames (budgeted navigation, returns per-frame ms); `cruciata.step` keeps its fixed navigation work limit. `cruciata.internals()` exposes `groundFree`, `nav`, `findFree`, `nearby`, `state`, `moveUnit`, `formationRoutes`, `combatAI`. Scripts: `scripts/stress-orders.js` (order latency and frame cost), `scripts/profile-order.js` (CPU profile of one order), `scripts/fuzz-movement.js [levels] [trials] [group] [seconds]` (random armies and destinations; reports units that never reach their slot), `scripts/balance-sim.js` (idle-player pressure timeline), `scripts/shot-battle.js` and `scripts/shot-walls.js` (screenshots).

**Freeze and hitch fixes.** Route smoothing in `findRouteSteps` bounds its look-ahead to 48 route points and yields inside the loop (an unbounded version once froze a level-3 frame for 10 s); the optional detour pass of `gapRouteSteps` is capped at 160 waypoints (the unreachable-start/goal pass keeps 512). `moveUnit` memoizes line-of-sight (`clearCached`, 0.2 s per unit and slot), limits the route-join scan to 12 points within 900 units, and skips cohesion and shortcut checks beyond 320/480 units. `curtainBlocks` rejects pieces beyond 42 units immediately. Combat target scans are staggered (`u.acquireAt`, 0.2-0.45 s; idle troops scan at once), civilians re-plan flight twice a second (`u.fleeAt`), and spatial grids use integer `cellKey` keys. Destroying a building sets `state.navCombat`; physical collision updates instantly through the `hp` check and the route grid rebuilds at most every 0.3 s (`state.navAt`). The watchdog accepts a stalled arrival only within 40 units, or within 150 after 7.5 s of stall.

**Alerts and controls.** `raiseAlert` (from `damage`) pings the minimap (`state.alerts`, 8 s), shows a toast and horn at most every 10 s when the attacked player entity is off-screen, and `Z` (`jumpToAlert`) centers the camera on the latest alert. Double-clicking a unit selects every unit of its type on screen. Optional state fields (`alerts`, `alertAt`, `debris`, `navCombat`, `navAt`, unit `progress`/`runUp`/`acquireAt`/`fleeAt`) initialise lazily, so older saves load unchanged.

**Map generation guards.** `nearBridge` keeps independent-city masonry off the river bridge (small river maps previously had a closed neutral gate on the only crossing); `placeClear` resolves overlaps when the winter castle capital is enlarged. `node scripts/map-sanity.js` sweeps quickmatch biomes and seeds for connected bases.

**Diagnostics.** `cruciata.phases()` returns per-phase update milliseconds since the last call (test builds only). `scripts/spike-hunt.js`, `freeze-hunt.js` (slow navigation jobs), `phase-hunt.js` (slow frames by phase), `spike-profile.js` and `profile-live.js` (CPU profiles of slow frames/windows) and `profile-sim.js` find hitches; `shot-siege.js`, `shot-ui.js` capture visuals. Test files that draw atlas-backed art must warm it first (`draw`, `await CruciataAtlas.ready`, draw again) because pages decode on demand.

Verification for this pass: `npm run check`, `npm run build`, and the full `npx playwright test` suite in chromium (206 tests) and firefox. `tests/responsiveness.spec.js` covers first-frame order response, committed moves, curtain footprints at all headings, double-click selection, cavalry charge and attack alerts.
